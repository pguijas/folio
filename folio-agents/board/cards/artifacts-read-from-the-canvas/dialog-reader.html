Dialog reader

The card is a mail and its attachments open inside it. Pick one and the dialog widens into letter and reading surface; close it and the letter is back. Nothing leaves the canvas.

What this variant argues

Thesis

Everything about a card, including what it produced, reads in one surface. The dialog is already where a card is read entire — header, letter, trail, band — so the attachment opens inside it: the dialog widens, the letter compresses to a summary plus the band, and the reading pane sits beside it. There is no second place to learn.

The band

Previous and next walk the card's attachments without going back to the letter, and the band stays on the left with the open one marked, so position in the band is always visible. Closed doors — targets nothing publishes — sit in the same band as kind, label and path, unclickable: the band is the card's full record, not just its readable part.

The docs page

If this wins, /docs/kanban/<id>/<stem>/ stops being the reading surface. The reading position here is a URL (#card=…&a=…), so deep links point at the board itself. What must keep existing is the published file under /_folio/kanban/… — the iframe and "open full" read it, and an agent can fetch it. The compiled docs page, with its sidebar and breadcrumbs, can go; at most it survives as a redirect for links already in the wild.

What it is bad at

A dialog is a cramped room for 8,000 words: the reading pane gets at most two thirds of the screen, and the embedded prototypes — pages built for full width — render at a width nobody uses them at. It blocks the canvas it claims to preserve: while you read, the board behind is inert, and you cannot compare an attachment with a card or with another attachment. And the letter reshapes itself the moment you open one — the surface stays one, but it does not stay still.

Keyboard: Tab reaches every card, Enter opens it, Enter on an attachment opens the reader, ←→ walk the band while reading, Esc unwinds one level and puts focus back where it was.
