    WHAT THIS VARIANT ARGUES

    Thesis

    Decomposition deserves a dedicated surface, so the hierarchy lives in a persistent outline rail (title and a status dot, nothing else) and the right pane renders whatever you select: a parent as a heading over a real table of its direct children, a leaf as the card itself. Navigation is the product. You drill into a subtree without losing your place in the whole, which is the only shape here that still works at ten times this board's size.

    The cross-column child

    theme-contract-for-plugin-surfaces sits in Backlog under a parent in In review. This view answers it by demoting status: in the rail the column is a coloured dot, an attribute of a node, never the thing that groups nodes. The tree is the only spine. So the cross-column child is not an anomaly to reconcile, it just carries a different dot.

    It is still worth flagging, so a child whose column differs from its parent's gets a divergence mark: a warning ring around its dot in the rail, and an explicit ≠ IN REVIEW badge in the status cell of the children table, counted in the heading. Divergence is annotated, not hidden and not smoothed away.

    Tree filtering

    Parents stay as context. A match keeps its whole ancestor path, and those ancestors render dimmed with a path mark so you can tell a hit from the road to a hit. Paths to matches auto-expand; the subtree under a match stays available.

    Promoting matches to the root was the alternative and it is wrong here. In a navigation surface the path is the information; a rail that reshuffles its own hierarchy per keystroke destroys the one thing it is for. The count under the search box keeps it honest: matches, and how many extra rows the context rule is costing you.

    What this is bad at

    It shows less at once than any other variant here, by construction. One parent's children at a time; comparing two cards under different parents is two selections and a memory. The board shape is gone, so you cannot see the columns fill up, you cannot see a WIP limit under pressure, and "what is in review right now" is a question this layout answers worse than the plain board does.

    The rail also spends a permanent 320px on ~35 rows of title text, which is a lot of chrome for a board this small. It earns that only if the hierarchy gets deep and wide. And the 10 childless top-level cards pay the full cost of the two-pane model while getting nothing from it: for them this is a list with an expensive left margin.
