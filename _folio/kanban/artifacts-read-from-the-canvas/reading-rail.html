Reading rail

Opening an artifact slides a reader in from the right. The columns compress but stay fully live: scroll them, filter them, open another card, and the rail retargets without closing.

What this variant argues

Thesis

Reading and the board share the screen: an artifact opens as a right rail beside the columns, the canvas never disappears, and the board stays live enough to filter, scroll and open other cards while the document holds still.

The dialog folds into the crumb

When the rail opens, the card dialog closes and collapses into the crumb in the rail header. One reading surface at a time: a dialog left floating beside the rail would be two overlays fighting over half a screen. The crumb reopens the dialog, and Esc in the rail does the same, landing focus back on the artifact tile that opened the reader.

The compiled /docs page

The board URL becomes the address of a reading position (#art=…), so the /docs/kanban/… page stops being where reading happens and leaves the docs sidebar. A deep link into a long document still wants a full window, and the page survives demoted to exactly that: the target of Open full, chrome kept minimal, indexed nowhere.

What it is bad at

Half a screen is a poor page. An 8k-word document reads in a 45%-viewport measure, and prototypes built for 1440px embed at roughly 600px, so the rail under-sells exactly the artifacts this card compares. The compressed board keeps two or three columns in view and grows a horizontal scrollbar, two scroll regions share one wheel, and board × dialog × rail has more state corners than a single modal ever has.
